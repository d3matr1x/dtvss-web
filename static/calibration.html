The k = 15 amplification policy

The DTVSS score multiplies a vulnerability's base patient-risk score by a temporal amplifier, (1 + k·L(t)), where L(t) is the daily EPSS probability that the flaw will be exploited. The constant k sets how hard that amplifier pulls.

How DTVSS uses EPSS as a continuous amplification factor →

k = 15 is set as an explicit policy, not fitted to the data. It states a risk appetite: a vulnerability at even-odds exploitation likelihood has its base patient-risk score amplified 8.5-fold. The 96-CVE filing dataset is then used to confirm that this policy produces a sensible distribution — not to solve for the value.

The amplification policy

The temporal factor (1 + k·L) is a multiplier on the base patient-risk score. With k = 15 it runs from ×1 when exploitation is implausible (L = 0) up to ×16 when exploitation is near-certain (L = 1.0). At L = 0.25 the multiplier is ×4.75; at even odds (L = 0.5) it is ×8.5.

Multiplier 1 + 15·L No amplification (L = 0) Even odds (L = 0.5)

L(t) is the EPSS probability of exploitation in the next 30 days, refreshed daily from FIRST.org. Because it moves every day, the score moves every day — the amplifier is the mechanism that makes DTVSS a temporal score rather than a static one.

Why 15

k controls the trade between two failures. Too low, and an imminently exploitable flaw never rises out of the pack. Too high, and everything carrying any exploitation signal saturates at the top and the scale stops discriminating. k = 15 places the turning point so that even-odds exploitation is enough to carry a high-harm device into the Critical band, while low-exploitability findings stay low.

It is a policy — set once and stated — rather than a number solved for against the dataset, and that is deliberate. An amplification constant should not move when one CVE's exploitation probability is revised, and EPSS values are revised constantly. Solving for the value that places a single named CVE exactly at a threshold makes the constant a hostage to that CVE's daily EPSS score. DTVSS states k, and uses the data to check it.

A worked example

CVE-2017-12718, a buffer overflow in the ICU Medical Plum 360 infusion pump (Class IIb, so H = 7.5), with CVSS exploitability sub-score B = 2.20. Applying the policy at an EPSS reading of L = 0.2577:

WORKED EXAMPLE · ONE EPSS SNAPSHOTDTVSS = (2.20 / 10) · (7.5 / 10) · (1 + 15 · 0.2577) · 10 = 1.65 · (1 + 15 · 0.2577) = 1.65 · 4.866 = 8.03 CRITICAL

Time-relative. L = 0.2577 is one day's EPSS reading; it drifts as FIRST.org re-estimates exploitation likelihood, and the score drifts with it. This shows how the policy scores a real flaw on one day, not a fixed property of the CVE. At even odds (L = 0.5) the same base would amplify to 1.65 · 8.5 = 14.0 before the score is shaped toward the 10-point ceiling (below).

The top of the scale

The amplifier can drive the raw product well past 10 — an even-odds flaw on a Class III device clears it easily. Hard-clipping every such finding to a flat 10.0 would erase the difference between a serious vulnerability and an extreme one. Instead, DTVSS shapes the top of the range smoothly: scores below 8.0 are exact, and above 8.0 the overshoot is compressed asymptotically toward 10 without ever reaching it. An exact 10.0 is reserved for one case — a CVE confirmed in active exploitation (CISA KEV), which is forced to 10.0 regardless of k.

Validation on the filing data

The stated policy is checked against the 96-CVE filing dataset (87 Class IIb, 9 Class III), frozen at the time of patent filing. These checks confirm the distribution is sensible — they validate k = 15, they do not derive it.

Corpus-independent. Because k is stated rather than fitted, it applies unchanged to any set of CVEs — nothing about the value depends on these 96. Against a live EPSS feed the absolute scores move daily by design, and the share reaching Critical can drift as exploitation estimates change; managing that share is the job of an operational triage layer, not a reason to re-fit the constant.

Reproduce it

All inputs are public. The check runs offline in under a second against the committed dataset, using only the Python standard library. To re-validate against a live snapshot, the same script fetches current EPSS from FIRST.org with the --live flag.

$ git clone https://github.com/d3matr1x/dtvss-web.git && cd dtvss-web $ python3 calibrate_k.py → k = 15 (stated amplification policy) → Validation on filing dataset: Critical 5.21% low-risk 0.98 span 9.44 [PASS]
Checked on every commit.
A continuous integration workflow runs calibrate_k.py against the frozen 96-CVE dataset on every push and pull request, and asserts that the calibration record in results.json — the stated k and its validation figures — reproduces exactly. Drift breaks the build. View CI runs →
